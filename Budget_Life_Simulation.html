Real-life choices • A classroom simulation

A Month in Someone Else’s Shoes

One household. One month. Many decisions. Build a budget, respond when life changes, and discover why everyone’s choices look different.

Teacher guide & classroom plan

Three class periods • 22 individual profiles

Before class: Assign each student a different profile number, 1–22. Use “Print all profile cards” for paper copies. All people and amounts are fictional instructional estimates set in Northern Utah, 2026; these are not verified local prices, salary averages, tax calculations, or benefit eligibility estimates.

Day 1 — Meet your household: Read the profile. Use monthly take-home pay, not annual gross income. Fixed bills are already entered. Adjust the flexible categories, include savings, and explain trade-offs. Suggested starting amounts are discussion prompts, not minimum living standards.

Day 2 — Life Happens: Each student saves an initial budget, then draws one event when you give the signal. Students revise flexible spending or use available emergency savings. Drawing is locked after one event per profile. The teacher signal is a classroom instruction, not a password lock.

Day 3 — Compare and reflect: Pair contrasting households. Compare housing burden, childcare, transportation, savings and the same $400 unexpected bill. Discuss which constraints cannot be solved by cutting small optional purchases. A deficit is a discussion result, not a failing score.

Money rules: Take-home income is a supplied classroom figure after assumed taxes; insurance shown below has not been deducted from it. Insurance includes assumed health, auto where applicable, and renters coverage; mortgage housing costs include homeowners coverage and property tax. Car payments are in transportation; the separate debt line excludes cars and mortgages. Internet and phone are a separate flexible line. Savings transfers are allocations, not consumption. Emergency withdrawals are one-time funds, not income.

Assessment (10 points): Complete a realistic budget (3), explain two choices (2), revise and explain the event response (3), compare structural constraints respectfully (2). Do not grade students on having a surplus or ask for their real finances.

Saving and submission: Work saves on this browser when storage is available. It does not sync or send results to the teacher. Students should use “Print / save my work” to submit a PDF through your usual classroom system. Save before changing devices. On shared devices, reset the profile after submitting.
