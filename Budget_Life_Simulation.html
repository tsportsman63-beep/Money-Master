Budget activity • Northern Utah • 2026

A Month in Someone Else’s Shoes

Calculate your pay. Plan for your bills. Build a budget that reflects your household’s real constraints.

Teacher guide & classroom plan

Three class periods • 22 individual profiles

Day 1: Assign profiles 1–22. Students divide annual household wages by 12 and calculate employee Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%) and simplified Utah income tax (4.45%). A fictional monthly federal withholding amount is supplied, not calculated from a federal tax bracket. Students calculate total deductions and model take-home pay, then check their work.

Day 2: Finish a first budget, save it, and draw one Life Happens card on your signal. Students revise their spending and explain any unmet needs. Income calculations lock when the first budget is saved. Electricity, natural gas, and water/sewer/trash are supplied monthly amounts already included in the budget.

Day 3: Compare households. How would a $400 surprise affect someone with childcare expenses, a roommate, or little savings? A deficit can be a realistic result; assess reasoning rather than wealth.

Model boundaries: All profiles and supplied expenses are invented classroom assumptions. Treat all wages as covered W-2 wages with no pretax deductions. All household annual incomes here fall below the 2026 Social Security wage cap of $184,500, so combining household wages works for this simplified percentage exercise. Real payroll is calculated per worker. Utah’s 4.45% is the starting percentage in the current Publication 14; actual withholding applies allowances and wage adjustments. Federal withholding varies by earnings and W-4 choices. This model is not an actual payroll or tax calculator.

Expense rules: Insurance is paid from the model take-home amount; do not deduct it twice. Mortgage amounts include property tax and homeowners insurance. Car loan payments are in transportation, not other debt. Electricity, natural gas, and water/sewer/trash are listed as separate fixed monthly amounts. These invented classroom estimates already represent each household’s share, including shared housing. Fuel in the flexible budget means transportation fuel, not home heating. Utilities are included automatically; students do not need to research, calculate, divide, or enter utility bills.

Assessment (20 points): Deduction calculations and explanation (5); complete budget and trade-offs (6); event response (5); comparison and reflection (4). Calculator and payroll reference links open in new tabs for Canvas.

Submission: Print / save work creates a submission students can save as PDF. Progress stays on the same browser when permitted; it is not sent to the teacher or synced. Canvas may limit browser storage. This edition starts separate work and does not overwrite the earlier edition’s saved budgets. Submit before resetting on shared devices.
